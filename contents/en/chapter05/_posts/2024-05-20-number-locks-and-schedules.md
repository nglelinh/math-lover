## Hook

Two sprites dance in a game. The red one hops every 4 beats. The blue one hops every 6 beats. They start together.

When do they land on the same beat again?

That is the library-visit story from least common multiple — now it is a game clock.

## Discovery Puzzle

Red hops on 4, 8, 12, 16, 20…
Blue hops on 6, 12, 18, 24…

Circle every number that appears in both lists. What is the smallest circled number?

If a third sprite hops every 5 beats, will 12 still work for all three?

## Core Idea

### Shared beats

The first time two loops meet again is the **least common multiple**.

$$4 = 2 \times 2, \quad 6 = 2 \times 3$$

Keep the most of each brick: $$2 \times 2 \times 3 = 12$$.

### Shared packs

If 12 songs and 18 songs go into folders of the same size, the largest folder that works for both is the **greatest common factor**, 6.

### Why primes feel like locks

A prime has only the boring factor pair $$1 \times p$$. Computers sometimes lock a message by mixing two large primes. You do **not** need the adult details. The kid idea is enough: **a number made from huge prime bricks is annoying to un-bake**, the way 15 un-bakes easily into 3 and 5 but a giant mystery number does not.

## Guided Discovery Activities

### Activity 1: Hand-clap metronome

One person claps every 2 counts, another every 3. Together they meet every 6. That is LCM you can hear.

### Activity 2: Sticker packs

18 moon stickers and 24 star stickers. Largest equal packs with none left over? GCF 6.

### Activity 3: Tiny lock

Pick two small primes, say 5 and 7. The product is 35. List factor pairs of 35. Why are there so few?

## Example Walkthrough

Game events: fireworks every 8 seconds, drums every 12 seconds.

$$8 = 2 \times 2 \times 2,\quad 12 = 2 \times 2 \times 3$$

LCM: $$2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 = 24$$.

They line up every 24 seconds.

## Parent Corner

Keep “secret codes” playful. Do not introduce scary security jargon. The thinking skill is structure: primes, shared factors, shared multiples. Vietnamese Chapter 5 already teaches the tools; this English note only points to games and simple locks.
