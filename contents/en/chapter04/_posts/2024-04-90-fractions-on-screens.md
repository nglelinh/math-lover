## Hook

A game heart bar is not “a little full.” It is **3 of 4 equal blocks**.

A download bar that is three-quarters across the screen is the same fraction as three pizza slices of four.

Screens are full of fair shares you can *see*.

## Objectives

By the end of this optional note, learners can:

- read a progress bar as a fraction
- connect $$3/4$$ to 75% on a battery icon
- explain why the parts on a bar must be equal

You already cut wholes into halves, thirds, and fourths, and compared them.

Today the “pizza” is a rectangle on a phone: a loading bar, a health bar, or a battery.

## Discovery Puzzle

A battery icon is split into 4 equal blocks. Three blocks are green.

- What fraction is full?
- What fraction is empty?
- If the same battery later shows 2 green blocks, did the *whole* change or the *filled part*?

---

## Core Idea

A progress bar is a rectangle divided into equal parts.

$$\frac{\text{filled parts}}{\text{all parts}} = \text{the fraction you see}$$

Three of four:

$$\frac{3}{4}$$

The same amount as 75 out of 100, which batteries often write as 75%. You do not need a long percent lesson here — just notice they name the same share.

Close up, a screen is a grid of tiny color dots called **pixels**. Photographers and Wikimedia diagrams of pixel grids show why “half the picture” means half the dots, not a smear.

Equal parts matter here too. If a progress bar cheats and makes the last block twice as wide, “3 of 4” is no longer fair — just as unequal pizza slices are not true fourths. Apps that show 1/4, 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 as a growing bar are teaching equivalent steps toward one whole.

## Guided Discovery Activities

### Activity 1: Paper phone

Draw a rectangle. Cut it into 4 equal strips. Color 3. That is a homemade health bar.

### Activity 2: Pause a download

If a family download bar is halfway, ask “halfway of what whole?” The whole is the file, not the screen.

### Activity 3: Heart pieces

Many games show 5 hearts. Two empty hearts means $$3/5$$ health left. Write it; do not only say “almost dead.”

## Example Walkthrough

A song is 4 minutes. The bar is at 1 minute.

Filled share: $$\frac{1}{4}$$.

Two minutes: $$\frac{2}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$$.

Same song, different filled part — the denominator stayed 4 because the whole song did not change.

## Thinking Questions

1. Why is a bar that is “mostly full” not a number until we choose equal parts?
2. Can $$2/4$$ and $$1/2$$ look different on two apps but mean the same share?
3. If a heart bar has 5 hearts and you lose 1, what fraction remains?

## Parent Corner

This note sits beside the pizza wonder; it does not replace it. Screens give children daily fraction pictures. Ask “how many equal parts is the whole?” before reading any percent on a battery.
