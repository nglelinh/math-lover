## Hook

Which is larger: $$7.01$$ or $$7.1$$?

Many children pick $$7.01$$ because “01 looks longer.” That is a trap.

The point is a fence. We compare from the *left*, place by place — just as we do with whole numbers.

## Discovery Puzzle

Write these fractions as decimals:

- $$3/10$$
- $$7/100$$
- $$25/100$$

Then place $$0.3$$, $$0.07$$, and $$0.25$$ on a number line from 0 to 1.

Which one sits farthest to the right?

## Core Idea

A decimal is a short writing of a fraction with denominator 10, 100, 1000, and so on.

$$1/10 = 0.1$$ (one tenth)

$$1/100 = 0.01$$ (one hundredth)

$$3/10 = 0.3$$

$$45/100 = 0.45$$

### Two parts

In $$12.35$$:

- the **whole part** is 12 (left of the point)
- the **decimal part** is 35 (right of the point)

The 3 is in the tenths place. The 5 is in the hundredths place.

### Comparing

1. Compare the whole parts first.
2. If they match, compare tenths, then hundredths, then thousandths.

$$3.5 > 3.2$$ because 5 tenths is more than 2 tenths.

$$12.1 < 12.9$$ because 1 tenth is less than 9 tenths.

$$5.0 = 5.00$$ because the extra zeros are empty places.

$$7.01 < 7.1$$ because $$7.1 = 7.10$$, and 01 hundredths is less than 10 hundredths.

## Guided Discovery Activities

### Activity 1: Write the decimal

- $$3/10 = 0.3$$
- $$7/100 = 0.07$$
- $$25/100 = 0.25$$

Notice the zero in $$0.07$$. Without it, $$0.7$$ would mean seven tenths — ten times larger.

### Activity 2: Read them aloud

- $$5.4$$: five and four tenths
- $$12.08$$: twelve and eight hundredths
- $$0.75$$: seventy-five hundredths

### Activity 3: Compare

- $$4.3$$ and $$4.5$$ → $$4.3 < 4.5$$
- $$7.01$$ and $$7.1$$ → $$7.01 < 7.1$$

Line the points up. Add zeros so both numbers have the same number of decimal places. Then compare as whole numbers of hundredths.

## Example Walkthrough

Write $$47/100$$ as a decimal: $$0.47$$.

Write $$123/1000$$ as a decimal: $$0.123$$.

Compare $$12.1$$ and $$12.01$$.

$$12.10$$ versus $$12.01$$. Ten hundredths is more than one hundredth, so $$12.1 > 12.01$$.

Is $$0.5 = 0.50$$? Yes. Five tenths is fifty hundredths.

## Math Through Time

**Simon Stevin** (1548–1620), a Dutch mathematician, argued that decimal writing should replace awkward fraction arithmetic for everyday work.

Before that, many people still used sixtieths — a leftover from Babylonian time and angle measures.

Today you meet decimals on money (15.5 thousand dong), measuring tapes (1.5 m), kitchen scales (2.75 kg), and report cards (8.5 points).

## Parent Corner

The child is learning to *read places*, not to count how many digits “look bigger.”

Helpful prompts:

- "Line up the points."
- "Can you give both numbers the same number of decimal places?"
- "Show me tenths versus hundredths."

A meter stick marked in tenths of a meter, or a kitchen scale, makes the places real.

## Thinking Questions
