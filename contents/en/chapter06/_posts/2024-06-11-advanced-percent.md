## Hook

A bicycle costs 2,500,000 dong. It is 20% off.

A phone costs 8,000,000 dong. Next month the price rises 15%.

A savings book pays 8% a year for 2 years.

Three stories. Same tool: a percent of an original amount — then add or subtract, and sometimes multiply by years.

## Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners can:

- compute a new price after a percent increase
- compute a new price after a percent decrease
- compute simple interest over more than one year
- keep increase, decrease, and interest stories from mixing up

## Discovery Puzzle

A television costs 5,000,000 dong and rises 10%.

Is the new price 5,000,000 + 10, or 5,000,000 + 500,000?

Now try a shirt at 800,000 dong, 25% off. Predict before you compute.

---

## Core Idea

### Percent increase

New price = old price + (old price $$\times$$ percent / 100)

Television 5,000,000 dong, up 10%:

$$5000000 + (5000000 \times 10/100) = 5000000 + 500000 = 5500000$$

You can also think: the new price is $$110\%$$ of the old price.

$$5000000 \times 110/100 = 5500000$$

### Percent decrease

New price = old price − (old price $$\times$$ percent / 100)

Shirt 800,000 dong, 25% off:

$$800000 - (800000 \times 25/100) = 800000 - 200000 = 600000$$

Or: keep $$75\%$$ of the old price.

$$800000 \times 75/100 = 600000$$

### Simple interest over time

Interest = original $$\times$$ rate $$\times$$ years / 100

Save 10,000,000 dong at 7% a year for 1 year:

$$10000000 \times 7 \times 1 / 100 = 700000$$ dong interest

This lesson uses *simple* interest: each year pays the same percent of the starting amount. Compound interest — interest on interest — is a later adventure.

### Activity 2: Two years of simple interest

Save 5,000,000 dong at 8% a year for 2 years.

$$5000000 \times 8 \times 2 / 100 = 800000$$ dong interest

## Example Walkthrough

1,200,000 dong, 15% off:

Discount 180,000. New price 1,020,000.

3,600,000 dong, 10% up:

Increase 360,000. New price 3,960,000.

20,000,000 dong at 6% a year for 3 years:

$$20000000 \times 6 \times 3 / 100 = 3600000$$ dong interest.

## Math Through Time

Roman tax and interest tables already asked “how much per hundred, and for how long?”

Fibonacci’s merchant book brought those tables to Europe. Banks still use the same three ingredients: original, rate, time.

Businesses use percent for profit. Shops use it for sales. Families use it for savings. The stories change; the skeleton stays.

Level 3: A 2,500,000 dong bicycle is 20% off. A friend says the new price is 2,480,000. What mistake did they make?

## Parent Corner

The child is connecting *percent change* with *time*.

Helpful prompts:

- "Are we adding the change or subtracting it?"
- "What is the original?"
- "Is this one year or several years?"

A real sale flyer plus a toy “savings book” is enough. Stay with simple interest unless the child asks about compounding.

## Thinking Questions

1. How can “keep 75%” replace “take 25% off”?
2. Why do we multiply by the number of years in simple interest?
3. If a price rises 10% and later falls 10%, do we return to the start? Try 1,000,000 dong.
