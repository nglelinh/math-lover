## Hook

Buy more notebooks, pay more money. The two amounts rise together.

Ask more friends to help with a job, finish sooner. One amount rises while the other falls.

Those are two different friendships between quantities.

## Discovery Puzzle

3 notebooks cost 15,000 dong. What should 5 notebooks cost if the price per notebook stays the same?

Now try this: 4 people finish a job in 6 hours. If 3 people work at the same speed, will the job take more than 6 hours or less? About how long?

## Core Idea

### Direct proportion

Two quantities are in **direct proportion** when one grows and the other grows in the same ratio.

Stories:

- buy more → pay more
- bake more cakes → use more flour
- travel longer at a steady speed → cover more distance

A useful picture: $$y = k \times x$$, where $$k$$ stays the same. For notebooks, $$k$$ is the price of one notebook.

### Inverse proportion

Two quantities are in **inverse proportion** when one grows and the other shrinks so that their *product* stays the same.

Stories:

- more workers → less time (same job)
- faster speed → less time (same road)
- with a fixed amount of money, cheaper price → more items you can buy

A useful picture: $$y = k / x$$. For the job, $$k$$ is the total “person-hours.”

### How to tell them apart

| Direct | Inverse |
|--------|---------|
| up and up | up and down |
| down and down | down and up |

Do not decide from the first word of the story. Ask: “If this doubles, does that double — or halve?”

## Guided Discovery Activities

### Activity 1: Name the kind

- number of children and number of candies if each child gets the same number → direct
- number of workers and time to finish one job → inverse

### Activity 2: Direct story

3 notebooks cost 15,000 dong. What do 5 cost?

One notebook: $$15000 \div 3 = 5000$$

Five notebooks: $$5000 \times 5 = 25000$$ dong

### Activity 3: Inverse story

4 people finish in 6 hours. How long for 3 people?

Total work: $$4 \times 6 = 24$$ person-hours

3 people: $$24 \div 3 = 8$$ hours

![A rectangle whose sides can trade length](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fa/Rectangle.svg/960px-Rectangle.svg.png)

*Image: Wikimedia Commons — math diagram*

Think of a rectangle with area 24. If the width changes from 4 to 3, the length must change from 6 to 8.

## Example Walkthrough

5 eggs cost 20,000 dong. 8 eggs cost

$$20000 \div 5 \times 8 = 32000$$ dong. Direct.

6 people finish in 10 days. 3 people need

$$6 \times 10 \div 3 = 20$$ days. Inverse.

Speed and time for a fixed trip are inverse: go twice as fast, take half as long.

## Math Through Time

**Archimedes** used proportions to compare areas and volumes.

**Kepler** used proportional thinking when he studied how planets sweep out space.

Builders still scale materials with direct proportion. Travelers still trade speed and time with inverse proportion.

## Parent Corner

The child is learning to ask *how two quantities move together*, not only to pick an operation.

Helpful prompts:

- "If we double this, what happens to that?"
- "Is the *product* staying the same, or the *rate* staying the same?"
- "What would break the story? Tired workers? A sale that changes the price?"

Cooking (direct) and car trips (inverse) are everyday labs.
