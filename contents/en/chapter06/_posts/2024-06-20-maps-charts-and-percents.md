## Hook

A paper map says **1 cm : 100 m**.

Your finger travels 3 cm. Your feet must travel 300 metres.

A phone pin uses the same idea: keep the ratio, change the size. GPS is not magic. It is careful measuring plus a scale.

![Map scale keeps a ratio]({{ site.baseurl }}/img/chapter_img/chapter06/90_map_scale.svg)

## Objectives

By the end of this optional note, learners can:

## Discovery Puzzle

Scale: 1 cm on paper = 200 m outside.

The park is 4 cm long on the map. How far is a real walk across the park?

If a news bar for “sunny days” is twice as tall as “rainy days,” does that mean twice as many days — or did someone pick a tricky scale?

## Core Idea

### Map scale

A scale is a ratio.

$$1\ \text{cm} : 100\ \text{m}$$

Three centimetres:

$$3 \times 100 = 300\ \text{m}$$

The pair stays in proportion. That is the same skill as recipe ratios and photo enlargements in this chapter.

### GPS pins, plainly

A phone estimates “how far from several sky helpers.” Then it marks a pin on a scaled map. You can think of the pin as two numbers (how far north/south, how far east/west) the way a grid names a square in a treasure map. No scary extra words needed. NASA Earth photos remind us the map is a tiny picture of a huge place.

![Earth seen from space — a giant map](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/The_Earth_seen_from_Apollo_17.jpg/960px-The_Earth_seen_from_Apollo_17.jpg)

*Image: NASA / Wikimedia Commons*

### Charts in the news

Math Antics’ *Data and Graphs* shows why bar height only means something if the axis starts in a fair place. A percent on a headline is “out of 100.” 40% of 20 children is 8 children — the headline is not the whole story until you know the whole.

## Guided Discovery Activities

### Activity 1: Room map

Measure a table in metres. Draw it so 1 cm = 20 cm in real life. That is a scale you chose.

### Activity 2: Weather percent

If an app says 70% chance of rain, that is 70 out of 100 similar days — not “it will rain 70 minutes.” (Chapter 7 goes deeper on chance.)

### Activity 3: Fair bars

Draw two bars: 4 books and 8 books. If you start the axis at 3, the “8” bar looks wildly taller. Talk about why news charts should start at 0 for counts.

## Example Walkthrough

Scale 1 cm : 250 m. Path is 2.4 cm.

$$2.4 \times 250 = 600$$ metres.

As a decimal, 2.4 cm is 2 cm and 4 mm. Lining up the point still matters.

## Thinking Questions

1. Why is a map without a scale only a picture, not a measuring tool?
2. Can two percents look equal in headlines but hide different group sizes?
3. If you zoom a digital map, what must stay in proportion?
