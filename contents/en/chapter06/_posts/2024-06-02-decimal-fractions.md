## Hook

A pizza is cut into 4 equal slices. You eat 1 slice. That is $$1/4$$.

A shop scale might write the same idea as $$0.25$$ of a kilogram.

Same amount. Two costumes.

You already know fractions and you have met decimal points.

Today we learn the *bridge*: divide to go one way, write tenths or hundredths to go the other.

## Core Idea

### Fraction to decimal

**Method:** divide the numerator by the denominator.

$$1/2 = 1 \div 2 = 0.5$$

$$3/4 = 3 \div 4 = 0.75$$

$$1/4 = 0.25$$

$$3/10 = 0.3$$

When the denominator is 10, 100, or 1000, the decimal is especially easy: the digits after the point *are* the numerator, padded with zeros if needed.

$$7/100 = 0.07$$

### Decimal to fraction

**Method:** write the decimal as a fraction whose denominator is 10, 100, or 1000, then simplify.

$$0.5 = 5/10 = 1/2$$

$$0.25 = 25/100 = 1/4$$

$$0.75 = 75/100 = 3/4$$

A balance scale is a good picture: both sides can look different and still weigh the same.

## Example Walkthrough

### Example 1

Change $$1/5$$ to a decimal.

$$1 \div 5 = 0.2$$

### Example 2

Change $$0.4$$ to a fraction.

$$0.4 = 4/10 = 2/5$$

### Example 3

Change $$7/4$$ to a decimal.

$$7 \div 4 = 1.75$$

The whole part is 1. The leftover $$3/4$$ is $$0.75$$.

## Math Through Time

For a long time, people preferred fractions. Decimal writing spread because it made lining up calculations easier.

In China, decimal-like place names appeared many centuries ago. In Europe, books after **al-Khwarizmi** helped the idea travel.

Money and measuring tools later made decimals everyday language: 2.50 on a price tag is fifty hundredths of a unit.

## Parent Corner

The child is learning that a fraction and a decimal can be *two labels on the same amount*.

Helpful prompts:

- "Divide the top by the bottom. What do you get?"
- "How many tenths is that?"
- "Can you simplify the fraction after you write it?"

Kitchen measuring cups (1/2 cup, 0.5 cup) make the bridge visible.

## Thinking Questions

1. When is a decimal easier to say than a fraction? When is a fraction clearer?
2. Why does dividing the numerator by the denominator give the decimal?
3. Why can $$0.25$$ become $$1/4$$ after simplifying $$25/100$$?
