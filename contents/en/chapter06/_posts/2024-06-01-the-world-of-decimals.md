## Hook

A juice bottle says 1.5 liters. A race time says 9.25 seconds. A test score says 8.5.

Those numbers are not “broken.” They are a new way to write parts of a whole — cousins of the fractions you already know.

By the end of this lesson, learners can:

## What We Already Know

You already know fractions such as $$1/2$$ and $$3/4$$.

Today we meet their “sibling writing”: a number with a **decimal point**. In English we write $$3.5$$. In Vietnamese you may also see $$3{,}5$$ — same idea, different mark.

## Core Idea

A **decimal** is a way to write a number using a point to separate the whole part from the part that is smaller than 1.

$$3.5 = 3 + 5/10$$

$$2.75 = 2 + 75/100$$

$$0.5 = 5/10 = 1/2$$

### Place values around the point

| Place | Value |
|------|--------|
| Tens | $$10$$ |
| Ones | $$1$$ |
| Tenths | $$0.1$$ |
| Hundredths | $$0.01$$ |
| Thousandths | $$0.001$$ |

Each step to the right is ten times smaller. Each step to the left is ten times larger. That is why we call them *decimal* numbers: *deci-* means ten.

### Why we need the point

Without a point, 35 could mean thirty-five ones. With a point, $$3.5$$ means 3 ones and 5 tenths.

The point is a *fence*. It tells us where the ones place sits.

### A tiny taste of operations

Line up the points, then add as usual:

$$12.5 + 3.25 = 15.75$$

$$2.5 \times 3 = 7.5$$

$$6.5 \div 2 = 3.25$$

We will practice those moves in later lessons. Today the job is to *see* what the digits mean.

## Guided Discovery Activities

### Activity 1: Same amount, two writings

Finish the matches:

- $$0.5 = 5/10 = 1/2$$
- $$0.25 = \ldots/100 = \ldots$$
- $$0.75 = \ldots/100 = \ldots$$

### Activity 2: Read it aloud

How do you say $$15.75$$?

“Fifteen and seventy-five hundredths,” or “fifteen point seven five.”

Both names are useful. The first one reminds you of the place values.

### Activity 3: A small add

Compute $$4.5 + 2.3$$ by lining up the points.

## Example Walkthrough

### Example 1

Read $$15.75$$.

15 is the whole part. 75 is seventy-five hundredths. So the number is fifteen and seventy-five hundredths.

### Example 2

Write “three point five” as a number and as a fraction.

$$3.5 = 3 + 5/10 = 7/2$$

### Example 3

Are $$0.5$$ and $$0.50$$ the same?

Yes. Five tenths is the same amount as fifty hundredths. The extra zero is an empty hundredths place.

## Math Through Time

Scholars in the Islamic world, including **al-Khwarizmi** around the year 820, wrote about decimal thinking. The English word *algorithm* comes from his name.

In the 1500s, **Christoph Rudolff** used a mark to separate the whole part from the fractional part. Different places chose different marks: a point, a comma, or a bar.

We use a base-ten system because humans have ten fingers. If we had eight fingers, school might teach “octal points” instead!

## Parent Corner

This lesson introduces decimals as *another writing of tenths and hundredths*, not as a new kind of number.

Helpful prompts:

- "Show me the tenths place."
- "Can you draw that as a fraction too?"
- "What would happen if we forgot the point?"

Look at labels on bottles, scales, and race times. Ask the child to name the whole part and the part after the point.
