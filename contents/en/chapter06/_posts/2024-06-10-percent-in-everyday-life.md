## Hook

A jacket tag says 150,000 dong. A sticker says “30% off.”

Is the new price 30,000? 120,000? 105,000?

Until you decide *what* the 30% is of, the sticker is only a riddle.

## What We Already Know

You already know that $$a\%$$ of $$b$$ is $$b \times a/100$$.

Today we put that tool into shops and savings jars.

## Discovery Puzzle

A shirt costs 200,000 dong and is 20% off.

First find the *discount*, then the *new price*.

Why is writing only one number on your paper dangerous?

---

## Core Idea

### Sales

Original price: 200,000 dong. 20% off.

Discount:

$$200000 \times 20/100 = 40000$$

New price:

$$200000 - 40000 = 160000$$ dong

The 20% is a share of the *original*, not a random 20,000.

### Simple interest

You save 1,000,000 dong. The bank pays 5% in one year.

Interest:

$$1000000 \times 5/100 = 50000$$ dong

After one year you have the original plus the interest: 1,050,000 dong.

For this lesson, “simple interest” means we compute one year’s percent of the starting amount. Later math studies interest that grows on interest.

## Guided Discovery Activities

### Activity 1: A jacket sale

Jacket 150,000 dong, 30% off.

Discount: $$150000 \times 30/100 = 45000$$

New price: $$150000 - 45000 = 105000$$ dong

### Activity 2: A savings jar

Save 2,000,000 dong at 7% for one year.

Interest: $$2000000 \times 7/100 = 140000$$ dong

### Activity 3: Estimate first

80,000 dong, 25% off. 25% is one quarter, so the discount is 20,000 and the new price is 60,000.

If your written work says 55,000, the estimate catches the slip.

## Example Walkthrough

100,000 dong, 10% off → 90,000.

80,000 dong, 25% off → 60,000.

50,000 dong, 40% off → 30,000.

1,000,000 dong at 5% → 50,000 interest.

500,000 dong at 8% → 40,000 interest.

## Math Through Time

Roman tax collectors already thought in hundredths. The English word *percent* still carries that Latin root.

Fibonacci’s *Liber Abaci* taught European merchants to compute interest and prices with hundredths — the same shop-math children still meet on receipts.

## Challenge Ladder

Level 1: New prices.

- 100,000 dong, 10% off
- 80,000 dong, 25% off
- 50,000 dong, 40% off

Level 2: Interest for one year.

- 1,000,000 dong at 5%
- 500,000 dong at 8%

Level 3: 80,000 dong, 25% off is 60,000, 55,000, or 20,000? Which wrong answers come from stopping too early?

Level 4: Two shops: 20% off 100,000, or 15,000 dong off 80,000. Which is cheaper? Why can a smaller *percent* sometimes beat a larger *dong* discount?

## Parent Corner

The child is practicing *three-box thinking*: original, percent change, new amount.

Helpful prompts:

- "What is the 20% of?"
- "Did you find the discount, or the price you pay?"
- "Can you check with 10% first?"

Read a real receipt together. Ignore extra fees for now; hunt only the percent and the original.
