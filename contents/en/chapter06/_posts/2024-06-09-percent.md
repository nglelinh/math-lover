## Hook

A weather report says “60% chance of rain.” A shop window says “25% off.” A coach says “we won 75% of our games.”

All three sentences use the same tiny language: **out of 100**.

## Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners can:

Today we give that special ratio a name and a symbol: $$\%$$.

## Core Idea

A **ratio** of $$a$$ to $$b$$ is $$a : b$$ or $$a/b$$.

Example: $$3 : 6 = 1 : 2 = 1/2$$

A **percent** is a ratio whose second part is 100.

$$25\% = 25/100 = 1/4$$

$$50\% = 50/100 = 1/2$$

$$75\% = 75/100 = 3/4$$

### Find a percent of a number

$$a\%$$ of $$b$$ is

$$b \times a/100$$

$$20\%$$ of 50:

$$50 \times 20/100 = 10$$

### Find the whole

15 is 30% of what number?

$$x \times 30/100 = 15$$

$$x = 15 \times 100/30 = 50$$

Think: 30% is 30 shares out of 100. If 30 shares equal 15, one share is $$1/2$$, so 100 shares equal 50.

## Guided Discovery Activities

### Activity 1: Compute

- $$10\%$$ of 200
- $$25\%$$ of 80
- $$50\%$$ of 150

$$10\%$$ is one tenth, so of 200 it is 20. That shortcut is worth keeping.

### Activity 2: A sale

A shirt costs 200,000 dong and is 20% off.

Discount: $$200000 \times 20/100 = 40000$$

New price: $$200000 - 40000 = 160000$$ dong

---

## Example Walkthrough

$$15\%$$ of 60 is $$60 \times 15/100 = 9$$.

A 100,000 dong item rises 10%.

Increase: 10,000. New price: 110,000 dong.

$$25\%$$ of 80 is 20, not 25. The 25 is the *percent*, not the answer.

## Math Through Time

The word *percent* comes from Latin *per centum* — “for each hundred.”

**Fibonacci** used hundredths in *Liber Abaci* (1202) after learning arithmetic on his travels. The $$\%$$ symbol grew later: from “per 100,” to “p 100,” to the two-circle mark we use now.

Romans already collected a 1/100 tax. Chinese merchants computed interest long before the symbol existed.

Today percent is the language of weather, sports, growth, and price tags.

## Parent Corner

The child is learning percent as *a ratio out of 100*, not as a button on a calculator.

Helpful prompts:

- "Can you draw a 10-by-10 grid?"
- "What is 10% first? Can you use that to find 20%?"
- "Is that the discount, or the new price?"

Look at a real sale sign together and name the original price, the percent, and the amount saved.
