## Hook

A class has 15 boys and 10 girls.

You could say “there are 5 more boys.” That is comparing by *subtracting*.

You could also say “3 boys for every 2 girls.” That is comparing by *dividing*. That second sentence is a **ratio**.

## Core Idea

A **ratio** compares two numbers by division.

Ways to write the same ratio:

- $$3 : 2$$ (read “3 to 2”)
- $$3/2$$
- $$3 \div 2$$

### Stories

15 boys and 10 girls:

$$15 : 10 = 3 : 2$$

2 cups rice and 4 cups water:

$$2 : 4 = 1 : 2$$

### Simplifying

Divide both parts by the same whole number.

$$10 : 15 = (10 \div 5) : (15 \div 5) = 2 : 3$$

$$20 : 30 = 2 : 3$$

The simplified ratio keeps the *relationship*. It does not keep the original counts unless you scale back up.

### A first look at percent

A **percent** is a ratio with a second part of 100.

$$25\% = 25/100 = 1/4$$

$$50\% = 50/100 = 1/2$$

$$75\% = 75/100 = 3/4$$

We will spend three later lessons on percent. Today, notice that 50% is another costume for $$1 : 1$$ of a half-and-half split.

## Guided Discovery Activities

### Activity 1: Find the ratio

12 children like math. 8 like literature. What is the ratio of math fans to literature fans?

$$12 : 8 = 3 : 2$$

### Activity 2: Simplify

Simplify $$20 : 30$$.

$$20 : 30 = 2 : 3$$

### Activity 3: A percent taste

What is $$25\%$$ of 80?

$$25\% = 25/100 = 1/4$$

$$80 \times 1/4 = 20$$

## Example Walkthrough

Simplify $$4 : 6$$.

$$4 : 6 = 2 : 3$$

Boys to girls is $$3 : 2$$, and there are 15 boys. How many girls?

One “share pack” is 3 boys. There are $$15 \div 3 = 5$$ packs. Girls: $$5 \times 2 = 10$$.

What is $$45\%$$ of 200?

$$200 \times 45/100 = 90$$

## Math Through Time

Mapmakers write scale as a ratio: $$1 : 100000$$ means 1 cm on the map stands for 100000 cm outside.

Cooks have used “one to two” long before they used the colon symbol.

Percents later became the shopkeeper’s favorite ratio because “out of 100” is easy to picture.

## Parent Corner

The child is learning to compare by *sharing out*, not only by finding a difference.

Helpful prompts:

- "Can you say that as 'for every'?"
- "What number divides both parts?"
- "If we keep the ratio and change one number, what happens to the other?"

Sale signs, recipes, and class votes are good hunting grounds.
