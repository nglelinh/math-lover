At lunch you pick **one** item: soup **or** salad **or** rice — not two mains at once.

When choices **do not overlap**, use the **addition principle**.

---

## Objectives

- spot “pick exactly one group” problems
- add the number of options in each group
- explain why multiplication is wrong here

---

## Core Idea

$$\text{ways} = \text{ways in A} + \text{ways in B}$$

Example: 2 soups and 3 salads, pick one starter:

$$2 + 3 = 5$$

Use **addition** when you see **OR**. Use **multiplication** when you see **AND then** (next lesson).

---

## Exercises

1. 3 hot dishes or 2 vegetarian dishes — pick one. How many?
2. 7 long-sleeve or 4 short-sleeve shirts — pick one. How many?
3. Is “5 shirts and 3 pants = 8 outfits” correct? Explain.
