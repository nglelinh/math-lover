Pick **2 fruits** from apple, banana, orange for a smoothie. Apple+orange is the same group as orange+apple — order does not matter.

This is a **combination**: choose a set, ignore order.

---

## Objectives

- separate “line up” from “pick a group”
- list pairs without double-counting
- see why combinations are smaller than permutations

---

## Core Idea

Choose 2 from {A, B, C}: AB, AC, BC → **3 pairs**.

If order mattered: $$3 \times 2 = 6$$ arrangements, but each pair appears twice → $$6 \div 2 = 3$$.

Safe method for kids: **list every pair by hand** on small problems.

---

## Exercises

1. Choose 2 friends from 3. How many pairs?
2. Choose 2 numbers from {1,2,3,4,5}, order ignored. List and count.
3. Gold–silver medals vs two equal prizes — how is counting different?

## Parent Corner

Combinations are tricky because we must **merge** AB and BA. Listing first prevents the “divide by 2” rule from feeling magical.
