## Hook

This chapter taught three superpowers that work together.

Chance says how likely. Data says what we counted. Charts and summaries say what the counts mean. Binary shows the same numbers wearing a computer costume.

## Discovery Puzzle

A box holds 5 red balls and 3 blue balls. You pick one.

At the same time, a class list of scores is 6, 8, 8, 9, 9, and a computer stores the number 13 as $$1101$$.

What is $$P(\text{red})$$? What is the mode of the scores? Why is the last 1 in $$1101$$ worth 1 and not 8?

Three different rooms — one habit: *count carefully, then name the pieces*.

---

## Core Idea

### Probability

$$P = \frac{\text{favorable}}{\text{possible}}$$

Coin: $$P(\text{heads}) = 1/2$$.

Die: $$P(\text{a 1}) = 1/6$$.

A 70% rain forecast is a long-run chance, not a promise about tomorrow.

### Statistics

- **Mean** = sum ÷ count (fair share)
- **Median** = middle after sorting
- **Mode** = value that appears most

Scores 7, 8, 6, 9, 8 → mean $$7.6$$, median 8, mode 8.

### Charts

- **Bars** compare
- **Lines** show change through time
- **Pies** show parts of a whole

Honest charts need a title, labels, and a scale that does not cheat.

### Binary

Only 0 and 1. Places are 1, 2, 4, 8, …

$$13 = 8 + 4 + 1 \rightarrow 1101$$

Computers like two sturdy states: off and on.

## Guided Discovery Activities

### Activity 1: Three flips

A fair coin is flipped three times. All three land heads.

$$P(\text{HHH}) = (1/2) \times (1/2) \times (1/2) = 1/8$$

That is rare — and still possible. Rarity is not impossibility.

### Activity 2: A list with a job

Heights we used before had mean $$148.6$$ and median $$148.5$$.

Why are those two so close? What kind of list would pull the mean far from the median? (Hint: add one giant number.)

### Activity 3: Pick the picture

For each question, name the chart:

1. “Which sport is most popular?”
2. “How did temperature change from Monday to Friday?”
3. “What share of the pie is swimming?”

## Example Walkthrough

### Example 1

Box: 5 red, 3 blue. One ball.

$$P(\text{red}) = 5/8$$.

### Example 2

Convert 20 to binary.

$$20 = 16 + 4$$, so $$10100$$.

### Example 3

Cars: 100, 150, 120, 180. Peak month is the last one. A line would show the dip in month 3. A bar would make month 4 tallest. Both are true.

## Math Through Time

Pascal and Fermat counted chances. Graunt and Nightingale counted people. Playfair drew the counts. Leibniz and Boole gave machines a two-digit alphabet.

Modern weather, games, medicine, and spaceflight still rent those four ideas. Mathematics did not stay in the textbook.

## Challenge Ladder

Level 1: A box has 5 red and 3 blue balls. Find $$P(\text{red})$$.

Level 2: For 8, 7, 9, 6, 8, 7, find the mean. For 12, 15, 14, 18, 16, 14, find the median.

Level 3: Convert 12 to binary and $$1100$$ back to decimal. Do you land on the same number?

Level 4: Invent one story that needs probability, one that needs a chart, and one that needs binary. Tell a parent which tool you would grab first — and why the other two tools would be the wrong first grab.
