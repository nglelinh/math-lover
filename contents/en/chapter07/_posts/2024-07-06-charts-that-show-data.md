## Hook

The same four numbers can wear three outfits.

Bars make a contest. A line makes a journey. A pie makes a pizza of shares.

Choosing the outfit is part of the mathematics.

## Objectives

By the end of this lesson, learners can:

- choose among bar, line, and pie charts

Favorite sports in one class:

- Soccer: 25
- Basketball: 15
- Badminton: 20
- Swimming: 10

Which picture would you draw first if your friend asked, “Who is winning?”

Which picture would you draw if your friend asked, “What fraction of the class chose swimming?”

## Core Idea

### Three jobs, three pictures

**Bar chart** — compare values side by side.

**Line chart** — show a trend through time.

**Pie chart** — show how parts make a whole.

### How to draw a bar chart

**Step 1.** Name the parts.

- Across: the groups (sports, days, names)
- Up: the amounts
- Title: what the picture is about

**Step 2.** Draw bars whose heights match the numbers. Equal widths. Equal gaps.

**Step 3.** Write labels and units so a stranger can read it without you in the room.

### A sports example

Soccer 25, basketball 15, badminton 20, swimming 10.

The soccer bar should reach 25. The swimming bar should reach 10. If swimming looks taller than soccer, the picture is lying.

## Guided Discovery Activities

### Activity 1: Hottest day

Weekly temperatures:

- Monday: $$25^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$$
- Tuesday: $$27^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$$
- Wednesday: $$24^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$$
- Thursday: $$26^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$$
- Friday: $$28^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$$

![A thermometer for a temperature story](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Thermometer.jpg/960px-Thermometer.jpg)

*Image: Wikimedia Commons*

Which day is hottest? A line chart would climb to Friday. A bar chart would make Friday’s bar tallest. Both are honest — they emphasize different sentences.

### Activity 2: Books read

Draw a bar chart:

- Lan: 5 books
- Minh: 8 books
- Ha: 6 books
- Anh: 4 books

Check: Minh’s bar must be twice Anh’s bar, because $$8 = 2 \times 4$$.

### Activity 3: Would a pie fit?

Add the sports votes: $$25 + 15 + 20 + 10 = 70$$.

Swimming’s share is $$10/70 = 1/7$$. A pie can show that slice. A bar still compares more quickly.

## Example Walkthrough

### Example 1

Cars sold: January 100, February 150, March 120, April 180.

April is the peak. A line chart would dip after February, then climb to April. That dip is a fact the bars also know — the line just makes the journey obvious.

![A line chart for a journey through time](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/16/Line_graph.svg/960px-Line_graph.svg.png)

*Image: Wikimedia Commons — math diagram*

### Example 2

Five quiz scores: 7, 8, 6, 9, 8. Draw five bars, one per child. The 9-bar is tallest. The 6-bar is shortest.

## Math Through Time

**William Playfair** (1759–1823), a Scottish thinker, invented the bar chart and the line chart so business numbers could be *seen*.

Newspapers, science labs, and company reports still use his idea: a title, two axes, and an honest scale.

A tricky chart can shrink the axis or stretch one bar. The mathematics includes noticing that trick.

## Parent Corner

This lesson is about *presentation as honesty*. A beautiful chart that starts the axis at the wrong place can fool a grown-up.

Helpful prompts:

- "What question is this picture answering?"
- "Do the bar heights match the table?"
- "Would a line or a pie do a better job?"

Look at one chart in a newspaper or an app. Ask the child to find the title, the units, and the tallest value.
