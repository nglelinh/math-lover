## Hook

A news chart, a board-game die, and a light switch walk into the same afternoon.

One shows **how many**. One shows **how likely**. One stores **on or off**.

That trio is Chapter 7 in kid clothes. The core path already teaches chance, charts, and binary; this optional note ties those ideas to news, games, and daily screens.

## Core Idea

### Data in daily life

Data is a collected list. A tally or bar turns the list into a picture. Math Antics’ *Data and Graphs* is a clear tour: labels, scale, fair bars.

Weather apps, class polls, and step counters are data — not guesses.

### Chance in games

A fair coin has 2 faces. Heads is:

$$\frac{1}{2}$$

A fair die has 6 faces. A 4 is:

$$\frac{1}{6}$$

Games use chance so the next roll is a surprise. We talk about **fair objects**, not betting. Khan Academy’s probability intro stays at this picture level.

![A die — each face is one outcome](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/82/Dice.svg/960px-Dice.svg.png)

*Image: Wikimedia Commons*

### Computer bits

[CS Unplugged](https://www.csunplugged.org/en/topics/binary-numbers/) cards and Khan Academy’s binary video show that computers count with 0 and 1. Vietnamese Chapter 7 already teaches this in more depth. Here is the English peek:

$$1011 = 8 + 0 + 2 + 1 = 11$$

A bit is a pair, like even/odd leftover.

![Binary place values](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Binary_decomposition.png/960px-Binary_decomposition.png)

## Guided Discovery Activities

### Activity 1: Pocket data

Tally colours of 15 books. Draw bars. Title the chart.

### Activity 2: Fair spinner

Make a 4-colour spinner with equal slices. Chance of red is $$1/4$$ only if slices are equal.

### Activity 3: Paper bits

Use cards 8, 4, 2, 1. Build 13. Which cards are on?

## Example Walkthrough

10 flips: 6 heads, 4 tails.

Data: the tally 6 and 4.

Chance *before* a fair flip is still $$1/2$$. A short sample can wobble.

A computer can store each flip as 1 or 0.
