## Hook

Five quiz scores sit on the table: 7, 8, 6, 9, 8.

What is “the” score of the group? Is it the middle one? The most common one? The fair share if you poured all the points into one pot?

Statistics gives those three ideas different names — because they answer different questions.

## Discovery Puzzle

Here are five scores: 7, 8, 6, 9, 8.

Find three different “middles”:

1. Add them and share the total equally.
2. Line them up from smallest to largest. What sits in the center?
3. Which number appears more than the others?

Are the three answers the same? If they differ, which one would you put on a report card — and why?

## Core Idea

**Statistics** is the craft of collecting, analyzing, and presenting data.

### Frequency

**Frequency** is how many times a value appears. In 7, 8, 6, 9, 8, the frequency of 8 is 2.

### Mean — the fair share

$$\text{mean} = \frac{\text{sum}}{\text{how many numbers}}$$

$$(7 + 8 + 6 + 9 + 8) / 5 = 38 / 5 = 7.6$$

If every child received the same score and the total stayed 38, each would get 7.6.

### Median — the middle after sorting

Sort: 6, 7, 8, 8, 9.

The third number is 8, so the median is 8.

If there are two middle numbers (an even count), the median is the mean of those two.

### Mode — the favorite value

8 appears twice. Everything else appears once. The mode is 8.

A list can have no mode (every value appears once) or more than one mode.

## Guided Discovery Activities

### Activity 1: Ten heights

Heights in centimeters:

145, 150, 142, 155, 148, 151, 147, 153, 149, 146

Write them in order from shortest to tallest before you compute anything.

### Activity 2: The mean height

Sum:

$$145 + 150 + 142 + 155 + 148 + 151 + 147 + 153 + 149 + 146 = 1486$$

$$\text{mean} = 1486 / 10 = 148.6$$

So 148.6 cm is the fair-share height.

### Activity 3: Median and mode

Sorted list:

142, 145, 146, 147, 148, 149, 150, 151, 153, 155

Two middle numbers: 148 and 149.

$$\text{median} = (148 + 149) / 2 = 148.5$$

Mode: none — every height appears once.

The mean and the median sit close together. That is a clue the list has no wild outlier.

## Example Walkthrough

### Example 1

Scores: 8, 7, 9, 6, 8, 7.

Sum $$8 + 7 + 9 + 6 + 8 + 7 = 45$$. Mean $$45 / 6 = 7.5$$.

### Example 2

Numbers: 12, 15, 14, 18, 16, 14.

Sorted: 12, 14, 14, 15, 16, 18.

Median: $$(14 + 15) / 2 = 14.5$$.

Mode: 14 (it appears twice).

## Math Through Time

**John Graunt** counted London’s births and deaths and asked what the lists were trying to say.

**Florence Nightingale** (1820–1910) used careful counts — and pictures of those counts — to show that hospitals could save more lives by changing everyday conditions. Statistics was her megaphone.

Today the same tools sit in sports box scores, news charts, and the way websites watch which button people tap.

## Parent Corner

The child is learning that “average” is not one magic word. Mean, median, and mode are three different cameras pointed at the same list.

Helpful prompts:

- "Did you sort before you hunted for the middle?"
- "Is that the fair share, the middle, or the most common?"
- "Would one unusual number pull the mean away from the cluster?"

Sports pages and weather tables are full of these three ideas. Pick one list and compute all three together.
