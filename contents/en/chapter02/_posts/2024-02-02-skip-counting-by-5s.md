## Introduction

Do you have a piggy bank? Or maybe you've seen coins - like nickels or 5,000 VND coins?

A nickel is worth **5 cents**. And 5,000 VND coins are worth **5,000 dong**.

Let's say you have 7 nickels. How much money is that?

Instead of counting one by one (5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 5 + 5), there's a much faster way: **skip count by 5s**!

Let's try: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35

That's 35 cents! See how fast that was?

---

## Core Idea

When we **skip count by 5s**, we start at 5 and keep adding 5 each time:

- Start at 5
- 5 + 5 = 10
- 10 + 5 = 15
- 15 + 5 = 20
- And so on...

Here's the pattern we get:

$$5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50, 55, 60, \ldots$$

**Look at the last digit!** It always alternates between 5 and 0:

- 5, 15, 25, 35, 45, 55... (end in 5)
- 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60... (end in 0)

This pattern never changes! If you know this trick, you can always tell if a number belongs to the 5s pattern.

**The Secret Pattern:**
- Start at 5 (or 0, but we'll start at 5)
- Each step, add 5
- The last digit always alternates: 5 → 0 → 5 → 0 → ...

## Guided Discovery Activity

### Activity 1: The Nickel Race

**What you need:** 10 nickels (or any 5 of the same coin), paper

**What to do:**

1. Line up 5 nickels in a row
2. Count by 5s: "5, 10, 15, 20, 25 cents!"
3. Add 5 more nickels
4. Count again: "5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50 cents!"
5. Ask: "How much would 12 nickels be?" (Let them skip count to find out: 60 cents!)

**What they're learning:** Connecting skip counting to real money amounts

### Activity 2: The Clock Adventure

**What you need:** An analog clock (or a picture of one)

**What to do:**

1. Look at the numbers on a clock: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5... up to 12
2. Start at 12 and count by 5s as you point to each number:
   - 12 → 1 (that's 5 minutes)
   - 12 → 2 (that's 10 minutes)
   - 12 → 3 (that's 15 minutes!)
3. Keep going around the clock
4. Ask: "What time is it when the minute hand points to 6?" (30 minutes, or half past!)

**What they're learning:** Skip counting by 5s connects directly to telling time!

### Activity 3: The Five Finger Power

**What you need:** Your hands!

**What to do:**

1. Hold up 1 hand (5 fingers)
2. Count: "5"
3. Hold up 2 hands (10 fingers)
4. Count: "5, 10"
5. Keep going up to 10 hands (50 fingers!)
6. Ask: "If everyone in our family held up their hands, how many fingers would we have? Let's count by 5s!"

**What they're learning:** Seeing the pattern with groups of 5

## Example Exploration

Let's say you want to buy 8 pencils that each cost 5,000 VND. How much will you pay?

Let's skip count by 5s eight times:

- 1 pencil = 5,000 VND
- 2 pencils = 10,000 VND
- 3 pencils = 15,000 VND
- 4 pencils = 20,000 VND
- 5 pencils = 25,000 VND
- 6 pencils = 30,000 VND
- 7 pencils = 35,000 VND
- 8 pencils = 40,000 VND

**Answer: 40,000 VND!**

We skip counted by 5s eight times to get 40. That's the same as saying "8 times 5" or "8 × 5"!

See how useful skip counting by 5s is in real life?

## Parent Insight

### Why This Matters

Skip counting by 5s is one of the most practical skills in mathematics:

- **Money**: Counting coins, making change
- **Time**: Reading analog clocks (every number = 5 minutes)
- **Measurement**: 5s appear in many counting situations
- **Multiplication**: 8 × 5 is skip counting by 5s eight times
- **Patterns**: Children learn to predict number patterns

### How to Guide the Conversation

- **Point out patterns in daily life**: "Look at the clock! The 5 means 5 minutes past!"
- **Make it practical**: "We have 6 eggs in each carton. How many eggs is that?"
- **Play games**: Count by 5s while walking up stairs, or clapping

### What's Happening in Their Brain

Skip counting by 5s helps children:
- See larger number patterns beyond counting by 2s
- Understand that patterns can have different "steps" (2s vs 5s)
- Connect math to real-world tools (clocks, money)

## Thinking Questions

1. **"What comes next: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, ...? What do you notice about the last digit?"**
   - Focuses on the 5-0-5-0 pattern

2. **"If we skip count by 5s and reach 50, how many numbers did we say?"**
   - Helps them understand the relationship between the count and the total

3. **"What time does the clock show when the minute hand is on the 9?"** (Hint: 9 × 5 = 45, so 45 minutes past the hour!)**
   - Connects skip counting to telling time

## Extension Activity

**Challenge: The Fives Finger Multiplication**

Here's a fun trick for multiplying by 5:

**For 5 × an even number:**
- Example: 5 × 6
- Take half of the other number: 6 ÷ 2 = 3
- Add a zero: 30
- So 5 × 6 = 30!

**For 5 × an odd number:**
- Example: 5 × 7
- Take half of the number minus 0.5: 7 - 1 = 6 (or think: 7 - 0.5 = 6.5, but let's keep it simple!)
- Actually, let's use: 5 × 7 = 5 × (7 - 1) + 5 = 30 + 5 = 35
- Or just skip count: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35!

**Try these:**
- 5 × 8 = ? (Answer: 40)
- 5 × 9 = ? (Answer: 45)
- 5 × 4 = ? (Answer: 20)

Can you discover why the even number trick works?
