## Objectives

In this lesson, children will discover patterns in number sequences, learn to identify the "rule" that generates a pattern, understand the relationship between input and output in patterns, and use patterns to predict future numbers.

---

## Prerequisites

Before starting this lesson, children should be comfortable with:
- Basic addition and subtraction
- Multiplication as repeated addition
- Understanding variables from previous lessons
- Skip counting (by 2s, 5s, 10s)

---

## Introduction

Look at this sequence of numbers:

**2, 4, 6, 8, 10, ...**

What do you notice?

Each number is 2 MORE than the one before it! 

This is a **pattern**—a rule that tells us how the numbers change.

Now look at this one:

**10, 15, 20, 25, 30, ...**

What's the rule here? Each number is 5 MORE than the one before!

Patterns are everywhere in mathematics. Once you find the rule, you can predict what comes next!

Let's become pattern detectives!

---

## Core Idea

A **pattern** is a sequence where the same thing happens over and over. To find the rule, ask: "What changes from one number to the next?"

Let's look at different types of patterns:

**Pattern 1: Adding (Going Up)**

$$3, 7, 11, 15, 19, ...$$

Rule: Add 4 each time

- 3 + 4 = 7
- 7 + 4 = 11
- 11 + 4 = 15
- 15 + 4 = 19

What comes next? **23** (19 + 4)

**Pattern 2: Subtracting (Going Down)**

$$25, 22, 19, 16, 13, ...$$

Rule: Subtract 3 each time

- 25 - 3 = 22
- 22 - 3 = 19
- ...

What comes next? **10** (13 - 3)

**Pattern 3: Multiplying (Growing Fast!)**

$$2, 4, 8, 16, 32, ...$$

Rule: Multiply by 2 each time

- 2 × 2 = 4
- 4 × 2 = 8
- 8 × 2 = 16
- ...

What comes next? **64** (32 × 2)

## Guided Discovery Activity

### Activity 1: Pattern Detective Cards

**What you need:** Index cards with sequences written on them

**What to do:**

1. Create cards with these sequences:
   - 5, 10, 15, 20, ?
   - 1, 3, 5, 7, ?
   - 12, 10, 8, 6, ?
   - 3, 6, 9, 12, ?

2. Ask your child to find the rule for each
3. Ask them to predict the next number
4. Challenge: Can they create their own pattern cards?

**What they're learning:** They're practicing systematic analysis of sequences!

### Activity 2: Input-Output Machines

**What you need:** A simple "machine" (you can draw one or use a box with holes!)

**What to do:**

1. Explain: "This is a magic machine! You put a number in, and a different number comes out."
2. Show examples:
   - Input: 2 → Output: 5 (Rule: +3)
   - Input: 4 → Output: 7
   - Input: 6 → Output: 9

3. Ask: "What's the rule?"
4. Let them try: Input: 8 → Output: ?
5. Challenge: Can they figure out the rule for more machines?

## Example Exploration

Let's solve some pattern problems together:

**Problem 1:**

Sequence: **4, 8, 12, 16, ?**

Step 1: Look at how each number changes
- 8 - 4 = 4
- 12 - 8 = 4
- 16 - 12 = 4

Step 2: The rule is "add 4" (or +4)
Step 3: Next number: 16 + 4 = 20

**Answer:** 20

---

**Problem 2:**

Input-Output Table:

| Input | Output |
|-------|--------|
| 3     | 9      |
| 5     | 15     |
| 7     | 21     |
| ?     | 27     |

Step 1: Look for the relationship
- 3 → 9 (3 × 3 = 9)
- 5 → 15 (5 × 3 = 15)
- 7 → 21 (7 × 3 = 21)

Step 2: The rule is "multiply by 3"
Step 3: For output 27: 27 ÷ 3 = 9

**Answer:** Input is 9

---

**Problem 3:**

Find the rule and next number:

**1, 4, 9, 16, 25, ?**

Step 1: Try adding: 4-1=3, 9-4=5, 16-9=7, 25-16=9
- The differences are: 3, 5, 7, 9 (adding 2 each time!)

Step 2: The differences are increasing by 2!
Step 3: Next difference would be 11
Step 4: 25 + 11 = 36

**Answer:** 36

Wait—let's check another way:
- 1 = 1 × 1
- 4 = 2 × 2
- 9 = 3 × 3
- 16 = 4 × 4
- 25 = 5 × 5

So 36 = 6 × 6 ✓

There are often multiple ways to see a pattern!

## Parent Insight

### Why This Matters

Pattern recognition is one of the most important skills in mathematics:

- **Algebra foundation**: Patterns lead directly to algebraic thinking and functions
- **Problem-solving**: Real math is about finding patterns and using them!
- **Prediction skills**: Understanding patterns helps predict outcomes
- **Mathematical beauty**: Patterns show the elegance and wonder of math

### How to Guide the Conversation

- **Ask "what comes next?":** Always follow up a pattern with this question
- **Look for multiple patterns**: "Can you see it another way?"
- **Connect to real life**: "Can you find patterns in nature? In music? In games?"
- **Celebrate wrong guesses**: "Great try! Let's look more carefully..."

### What's Happening in Their Brain

Pattern recognition exercises:
- **Sequential thinking**: Understanding order and progression
- **Hypothesis testing**: Making predictions and checking them
- **Multiple perspectives**: Seeing the same pattern different ways
- **Abstract reasoning**: Moving from concrete to abstract

---

## Thinking Questions

Ask your child these questions to deepen their thinking:

1. **"What is the rule for this pattern: 2, 6, 10, 14, ...? What comes next?"**
   - This practices identifying and extending patterns

2. **"Can you create a pattern where the rule is 'multiply by 3'? What numbers would be in it?"**
   - This moves from analysis to creation

3. **"Look at this: 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, ... What do you notice? Can you find the rule?"**
   - This introduces the famous Fibonacci sequence!
